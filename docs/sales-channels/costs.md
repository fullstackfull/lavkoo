# Operational costs and profitability

No price lives in code or configuration. Every price is a **contract rule** entered by an administrator, versioned and kept in the database; a service nobody priced is reported as **missing**, never as free.

## Switches: the module ships off

Running the migrations starts nothing. Two independent switches in `config/operational_costs.php`, both **off** by default and read with `FILTER_VALIDATE_BOOLEAN` (`off`, `no`, `0`, a typo all mean off). A third setting, the purchase-order source, is described in *Sources of truth* below: it ships `off` too, has three states, and needs the master switch as well.

| Switch | Environment value | Off means |
|---|---|---|
| master | `OPERATIONAL_COSTS_ENABLED` | nothing writes the cost ledger on its own account: the scheduled `costs:refresh` and `costs:accrue-storage`, the same commands run by hand (they print one line, `Operational costs are switched off (OPERATIONAL_COSTS_ENABLED): nothing was calculated or written.`, and exit successfully), the sale-time recognition of goods sold (no FIFO consumption, no estimate, no cancellation reversal), the storage accrual, the automatic putting back of returned units, and everything the finance importer does to the ledger: it still **stores** every statement fact it reads, but it posts none of them, takes none back (a statement row an API fee repeats) and sets no invoice line against the statements (see below). Only a person's own action writes the ledger while it is off (below). |
| schedule | `OPERATIONAL_COSTS_SCHEDULE_ENABLED` | only has an effect while the master switch is on: the scheduler does not run `costs:refresh` and `costs:accrue-storage` (they still run by hand). `channels:prune` is channel housekeeping, posts nothing and stays on the schedule whatever either switch says. |

What a person enters stays available with the master switch off, because a person is deciding and nothing starts by itself: contracts, purchase invoices and their approval (which posts the lines, or sets a marketplace line that is a total for a period against the statements already in the ledger), opening stock and product costs (cost layers), reconciliation decisions, approving, disputing and reversing a ledger entry, and the return decisions of a person (see *Refunds and returns* below: writing off damaged units always works; putting units back needs a cost of goods that was recognised, so with the master switch off there is nothing to put back).

**Statement postings and the catch-up.** The stored statement event is the queue: a fee or adjustment says `received` until its cost is in the ledger and `applied` afterwards. While the master switch is off the events are stored (nothing is lost, and a statement the channel never sends again is kept) and no ledger entry is written. When it is switched on the postings that were skipped are made exactly once, by the next finance import run and by `php artisan costs:post-pending-fees [account]`; a second run posts nothing, because an event that is `applied` is no longer waiting and the ledger refuses the same net twice. The same run takes back the statement rows an API fee repeats and sets the approved invoice lines that bill those fees against the statements (see *One charge, one cost*), so an invoice approved while the module was off and its statements are one cost once the catch-up has run. The cost screens show a banner while the master switch is off, and another while only the schedule is off; the Sales Channels hub lists both switches with the server switches.

**Order of enabling** (see [operations.md](operations.md)): enter the contracts, purchase invoices or opening stock first; switch the master on (`config:clear`), run `costs:post-pending-fees` and `costs:refresh --days=365` once; then switch the schedule on.

## Contracts

A contract belongs to one company (`provider_code`: `torod_point`, `m5azn`, `trendyol`, `amazon`, a gateway code …) of one party type (fulfilment provider, carrier, marketplace, payment gateway, supplier, other), in one currency, with an effective-from/to window, a reference number and notes.

* **Versioned, never overwritten.** `draft → active → superseded/archived`. Only a draft is edited. An active contract changes only through *start a new version* (effective from a date, with a reason); the previous version stays as it was and keeps pricing everything dated inside its window. `contractFor(company, date)` returns the version in force on a date. Revisions are logged.
* **Rules** price one service each: `service_type` (a `CostComponent`), `calc_method`, parameters, optional conditions (zone, country, city, payment method, channel, service level), priority and validity.
* **Expected components** (contract setting): a component the contract is expected to price but has no rule for is **MISSING** (visible), not silently skipped.

### Fee types (`CostComponent`)

Product cost: `cogs`, `supplier_cost`, `inbound_freight`. Fulfilment: `receiving`, `picking`, `packing`, `order_processing`, `packaging_material`, `stickers`, `printing`, `shipping`, `extra_weight`, `zone_surcharge`, `return_fee`, `cancellation_fee`, `cod_collection`, `special_fee`. Storage: `storage_daily`, `storage_monthly`, `storage_per_unit`. Payment: `payment_gateway`. Marketplace: `marketplace_commission`, `fba_fulfillment`, `fba_storage`, `fba_return`, `marketplace_service_fee`, `marketplace_shipping`, `marketplace_refund`, `marketplace_adjustment`. `other`.

### Calculation methods (`CalcMethod`)

`fixed`, `percent`, `percent_plus_fixed` (with min/max), `per_unit`, `per_shipment`, `per_order`, `per_day`, `by_weight`, `by_volume`, `by_zone` (zone lists in Arabic and English spellings are matched after normalisation), `tiered_quantity`, `tiered_value`. Tiers must not overlap; a percentage cannot exceed 100; numbers are numbers. The validator refuses a malformed rule and never supplies a figure. A fact the rule needs and the caller does not know (a missing weight, an unknown city) makes the cost **missing with that reason**, not zero. A rule is written in its contract's currency, so the order value a percentage, a price tier or a minimum order value is applied to is first brought into that currency at the recorded rate (3% of a 375 SAR order on a USD contract is 3% of 100 USD); with no rate the cost is missing with the reason *no exchange rate available*.

## The ledger (`operational_cost_entries`) — one writer: `CostLedger`

* **States:** `estimated`, `actual`, `reconciled`, `missing`, `disputed`. A missing entry has a NULL amount.
* **One effective entry per service of a subject** (the *slot*): a better-known figure supersedes a weaker one (actual over estimate over missing) and a weaker one never displaces a better one, so an estimate and the real charge are never both counted. Superseded entries stay as history.
* **Idempotent:** the same computation or document is one row (`dedupe_key`).
* **Approved and reconciled entries are frozen.** A different charge for their slot (an actual figure, whatever its rank) is stored beside them as *disputed*, outside the totals, for a person to resolve; it is never dropped without a trace. A charge a person has contested is held the same way: a later automatic posting for its slot (a newer net of the order, say) is stored beside it as a disputed figure for a person and never takes its place; only a reconciliation's confirmation of the charge does. A mistake is reversed with an equal and opposite entry (which carries the reporting-currency amounts of the entry it cancels, so the totals in that currency net too), never edited or deleted.
* **Disputes.** Only a charge (actual or reconciled) can be contested; it leaves every total until settled and remembers the state it had. *The charge stands*: it counts again as what it was (nothing is added). *It does not stand*: it counts again only to be cancelled by one reversal, so the two net to zero and the total is never driven negative (the charge was already outside it while contested). For a newer figure kept beside an approved one: *it stands* — it replaces the approved figure for the slot (which is superseded, not edited); *it does not stand* — it is set aside and the approved figure remains. Every outcome is audited (through the platform's audit log, as above).
* **Historical costs never change when a contract changes:** an entry keeps the contract, version and rule that priced it, its calculation inputs, and (for another currency) the exchange rate and the base amount at the time.
* **Money** is exact decimal throughout (`Money`, brick/math): six decimals stored, half-up, two shown; shared charges are split with the largest-remainder method so the parts add up exactly. FX conversion uses a recorded rate; with none, the amount is **left out, never counted as zero**, and every view built on it says so (see *Profit* below).

## Product cost (what the goods cost)

* **Inventory cost layers (FIFO).** A purchase creates a layer at a location (idempotent by source reference). Buying stock is an inventory movement, **not** an expense. The cost of goods is recognised **at the sale** (`operational_costs.cogs_recognition`: storefront at delivery, Amazon and Trendyol at shipment), consuming the oldest layers. A cancelled sale gives its units back, and what was recorded for each of its lines (an estimate or the recognised cost) is replaced by a settled zero, so the ledger and its export stop counting it; nothing is deleted, the earlier entry stays as history.
* A sale for which the layers hold less than the quantity records the shortfall (reported, never costed at zero).
* A sale line that is not linked to a product has a **missing** cost of goods (reason `product_not_linked`), not no cost at all: the order's profit is provisional until the line is linked, and linking supersedes the missing entry with the estimate or the actual cost.
* **Dropshipping (M5AZN):** the supplier cost is the cost of goods, taken from the supplier's price at the time.
* **Transfers are not sales.** Moving stock from Torod Point to Amazon FBA moves layers between locations (`transfer`), keeps the unit cost and records no revenue and no COGS.
* **Amazon FBA** stock, storage and fees are kept apart from Torod's (own location; fees from the Finances API only).

## Shipments, storage, marketplace and payment costs

* **Shipments:** the fee for one shipment is priced once per shipment (never multiplied by the number of items) and allocated to its items by the basis the contract names (default by component: shipping/extra weight by weight, picking/receiving by quantity, otherwise value). A fee that cannot be allocated is kept apart as *unallocated*.
* **Storage** is accrued per day per location from the stock figure the location vouches for; a day with no trustworthy figure is a missing cost with that reason, never a free day. Amazon FBA storage is Amazon's real fee, not priced here.
* **Marketplace fees:** commission estimated from the channel's contract when one exists (otherwise missing); the statement's real charge replaces it. Postings for one order (a commission, then part of it returned with a refund) are netted into one actual, and that includes a fee a **refund** gives back: the fee lines inside an Amazon refund transaction and the commission credit of a Trendyol `Return` row are netted with the original charge of the same order and fee type, so a full reversal leaves a settled zero and a partial one the remainder, never the original charge plus a second positive cost. A refund row whose type nobody could classify stays held for a person and moves no figure. FBA fees can only come from the statement and are missing until it arrives. Payouts are **not revenue**; a commission invoice that bills commission already carried on the sale rows is a duplicate and is not counted again (see *One charge, one cost* below).
* **Payment gateway** fees are priced from the gateway's contract (cash and wallet carry none).
* **Cancelled sales** owe no commission or gateway fee that was only an estimate or a gap; a charge that really arrived (a cancellation fee, a commission actually billed) stays. A line whose units were all cancelled while the rest of the order stands, and a whole order that is cancelled, give their units back to the cost layers and the slot of each costed line holds a settled zero.

## One charge, one cost

A fee can reach the books by four roads: our estimate from the contract, the channel's API, a statement a person imported (`channels:import-statement`), and the channel's invoice. What is guaranteed, and what is not:

* **The canonical identity** (`ChargeIdentity`, the same in every importer: Amazon, Trendyol and the manual statement): the channel account, the order and its package (or, for a charge that belongs to no order, the settlement), the fee type, the currency and the amount through `Money` to six decimals, so `25`, `25.0` and `25.00` are the same amount. What was charged is stored as a hash on the event (`sales_channel_events.charge_key`, indexed); what it is about is its `external_reference`.
* **Same thing, same fee type, same amount: a duplicate.** A statement row that repeats a charge the API already gave is recorded as `duplicate` (naming the API event it repeats, `duplicate_of_id`) and is **not counted**. A report that names only an order is about any package of it, on either side: a row that names the package matches an API fee that names only the order, and the other way round, in whichever order they arrive. An API event can be claimed by one row only, so two identical rows are two charges (a fee per unit of a two-unit order) while the API reported one.
* **No ids on the row** (a statement that names no order and no settlement): it is a duplicate only when **exactly one** API charge of the same shape was posted the same day (a day either side). With several candidates, or none (the API may simply not have read that day yet), it is imported and counted, and marked `possible_overlap` so that a person sees it. This is **not** resolved by the system: the row is counted until a person looks, and an API charge that arrives later only marks it too.
* **The other way round** is the same match from the API's side: a statement imported first and the API reading the period later. A statement row that names the same thing stops counting (and a row that was already posted as a fee of no order we hold is taken back with an equal and opposite entry); one that names nothing is only marked `possible_overlap`. This is done where the ledger is written, so only while the cost module is on: with it off the rows wait, and the catch-up does it.
* **Not guaranteed.** Two sources that report a charge with a different amount (a rounded figure) are two charges. A row that names an order we do not hold, or no order, can only be matched by the settlement or by its amount and day. A fact stored under another identity formula (see below) is a different fact.
* **Where it shows.** The channel logs list the events with their outcome (`duplicate`, `possible_overlap`) and the hub counts them. `channels:import-statement` prints how many rows were skipped because the channel API had already given the same charge, and how many were imported but may overlap.
* **The estimate** and the real charge share one slot of the order, so the estimate is superseded by the actual figure from whichever road arrives first and the later roads do not add a second one (the ledger refuses the same net twice).
* **The invoice** is set against the statements, whichever came first, where it bills a fee as one total for a period: see *Invoices and reconciliation*. An invoice line about one order takes the order's slot like any other posting of it.
* **Identity formulas changed in this release and nothing is backfilled.** The identity of a Trendyol statement row (its amounts now go through `Money`), of a statement file row (`ManualStatementImporter`) and of an Amazon fact (its posted date is no longer part of it) is built differently from the earlier formulas, and the events carry a `charge_key` that earlier events do not have. The module has never been deployed anywhere, so no environment holds an event stored under an earlier formula. If one ever does, the settlement rows the finance sync reads again inside its 3-day overlap would not be recognised as the facts already stored and would be counted twice; a clean start (or a backfill written for that case) is the way, not this release.

## Invoices and reconciliation

A company's invoice (number unique per company and seller; duplicates are detected and refused until a person says it is not one) is entered with its lines; each line is linked to a shipment/order where possible; **approval** records the lines as actual costs (idempotent). A line about a **shipment** is a charge of its own: a shipment can be billed in several lines and a credit note is a line with a minus sign, so they add up; the first positive one retires the estimate (or the "missing" marker) of the same service, whatever the component, and the charge is split over the shipment's items by the component's usual basis (the configured default; a contract's own `allocation_basis` setting only applies to the estimate). A line about a **marketplace order** takes the slot the channel's own figure uses (the estimate and the statement figure), so the same commission seen as an estimate, in the statement and on the invoice is one cost; a commission and its refund on one invoice are netted. A marketplace invoice line that is a **total for a fee and a period** with no order on it (the usual monthly commission or service fee line) is set against what the channel's statements (the API's, or a file a person imported) booked for that fee, currency and period, **whichever came first**: statements first, the line is compared at approval and posts nothing; invoice first, the line is posted in full (the statements had booked nothing), and as the statements are posted (the finance import, the catch-up after the cost module was switched on, a statement file) every approved line their days fall in is set against them: its full posting is cancelled with an equal and opposite entry and the line is compared like any other. The comparison (basis `statements`, the same tolerance) is a match within the tolerance and a variance for a person beyond it; while a variance is open the statements are what is counted. What the statements booked is told day by day, by the day each fee was posted (not the latest posting of its order, so a credit of the next month does not move the commission out of the month it was charged in), and a charge a person reversed is taken off again: a fee that was booked and given back is a total of zero, not "nothing booked". Accepting or writing off the difference books **only the difference**, as an adjustment of the same fee (a `marketplace_*` entry, source `invoice`); disputing it books nothing; and an accepted difference follows the statements that arrive afterwards (the adjustment is reversed and the new difference booked), so the books keep saying what the invoice says. A line the statements say nothing about is posted as before. Approving an invoice says how many of its lines were compared instead of posted, and *Reconcile* on the invoice sets its lines against the statements again (after a person reversed a fee, for instance). **Reconciliation** compares each billed line with the estimate for the same service within a tolerance (`operational_costs.reconciliation.tolerance_percent`, default 2 %), lists billed-versus-estimated differences and estimates that were never billed, and each difference is resolved by a person (accept the invoice, dispute it, or write the difference off; there is no "keep the estimate": approving the invoice already stops its estimate from counting). Voiding requires a reason. Everything is audited, through the platform's audit log (which records nothing on an installation whose database has no `audit_logs` table: see operations.md).

## Profit

For each sale: revenue (storefront orders; channel orders — mapped internal orders are excluded so nothing is counted twice) less product cost, fulfilment, storage, marketplace and payment costs. Revenue is without tax: a refund (which the platform records with the tax of the line in it) takes back the same share of the revenue as of the tax-inclusive line and never more than the line earned; a channel that gave VAT-inclusive prices with no VAT amount leaves the VAT in the revenue and the sale says so (unverified). A sale of goods that nobody has costed yet (no cost-of-goods row at all, not even a "missing" one) is **not** a sale that costs nothing: its cost of goods is named missing until the refresh has looked at it. The refresh (`costs:refresh`) looks at every sale placed **or changed** in the last days, so goods delivered or shipped days after the order are recognised then. **Estimated and actual profit are separate**, each with a completeness flag and the counts of missing, pending and disputed costs; a missing cost is never treated as 0. Reports group by product, order, channel, provider, and date; refunds reduce revenue; Trendyol/Amazon payouts are not revenue.

**An amount with no exchange rate** (a sale in EUR when the store records no EUR rate, a cost in another currency) is left out and recorded, with its currency, on the sale, the product row, the channel/provider row and the total (`ConversionGaps`): a figure built on it is **incomplete**, whatever else is complete. The by-product view and the by-sale view follow the same rule, so they agree: a row shows the figures that could be computed, flagged *incomplete: no exchange rate for EUR*; a row whose revenue is entirely unconvertible shows its revenue and its profit as **unknown** (a dash on a page, an empty cell in the CSV), never as 0; totals inherit the flag of their rows. The profit CSV carries, per line, the flag, the number of revenue amounts and cost entries left out and the currencies, and ends with a line that says how to put it right: record the exchange rate in the store's currency settings and export again (the same books are then complete). A cost nobody could determine (*missing*) and a charge in dispute (in neither cost figure) are counted apart, in their own columns, and are never confused with an amount that has no rate.

## Refunds and returns

Money given back and goods coming back are two facts, kept apart because they move different figures: a **refund** reduces the revenue of the order line it is about; a **return** is a decision about units, and only that decision moves stock and the cost of goods.

### Refunds reduce revenue (Amazon and Trendyol alike)

`ChannelRefundApplier` takes what a channel's refunds gave back off the revenue of the order line they are about. The amount is stored on the line (`external_order_lines.refunded_total` and `refunded_tax`, nullable decimals, NULL until a refund is read) and read by `SaleDocumentReader`.

* **Net of tax.** The goods, the shipping and a promotion that was taken off reduce the revenue; the tax that went back is recorded beside it, never taken off the revenue again.
* **Never more than the line earned.** The cap is applied where the revenue is read, so a refund that exceeds what the line earned (the shipping refunded again, an adjustment larger than the line) cannot drive the line's revenue below zero. When a line has a refunded amount in money it is that amount that is taken off, not also the value of its returned units. A line that only has returned units (a Trendyol claim) is valued at its units' share of the revenue.
* **Once per refund.** The amount is *set* per refund and refunded item (never added), so reading a refund twice, after a deferred transaction was released or after it changed, leaves the line right; two refunds on one line add up; a **partial** refund reduces the revenue by its amount only.
* **A refund with no physical return** (a price adjustment, goodwill money) reduces the revenue and nothing else: the cost of goods stays, because no unit came back.
* **A refund of an order we do not hold** is kept for review (`needs_review`, `order_not_held`), counted in no total or profit, and tried again on every run, so it is applied when the order arrives. A refund whose item matches no single line of the order (by SKU), or that is in another currency than the order, is kept for a person the same way and is never guessed onto a line.
* Refunds are order data, not ledger entries, so applying them does not depend on `OPERATIONAL_COSTS_ENABLED`.
* **Amazon.** Which breakdown of a refund is the goods, the shipping, a promotion or the tax is configuration (`amazon.refund_parts` in `config/sales_channels.php`, patterns on the breakdown type) because Amazon publishes no closed list; an amount that matches none stays unclassified for a person. **The sign convention (money back to the buyer is negative), the identifiers a refund carries and the classification have not been checked against a real Amazon refund**; check the first one (see the limits below).

The fees a refund gives back are netted against the original charge: see *Shipments, storage, marketplace and payment costs*.

### Returned units: put back on the shelf, or written off

A channel telling us that units came back (Trendyol: the units of a claim the channel **accepted**) does not move stock or the cost of goods by itself; someone has to look at the parcel. That is a deliberate, audited decision for each order line and return event (`ReturnDecisionService`, table `operational_cost_return_decisions`), made at the order page of the sales channels (the *Returned units* card), at the profit page of the sale, or at *Operational costs → Returns awaiting a decision*. The overview shows how many lines wait, and the orders list filters on *Awaiting a restock decision*.

* **Put back on the shelf.** The units go into a new FIFO layer at a location of the order's seller that the person names (by default the one the line was sold from), at the cost they left at (taken from the layers the sale consumed), and that part of the line's cost of goods is reversed in the ledger (`SaleCostService::restock`), so the profit of the sale follows.
* **Written off.** Damaged units create no layer and no entry: their cost of goods **stays** in the profit.
* **Once per line and return event.** The event is the claim (`claim:<id>`) or, for a line the channel reports no returned units for, a return the person recorded (`manual:<units already decided on the line>`). Deciding the same event again changes nothing and says so.
* **A claim that grows.** If the channel accepts another item of a claim after it was decided, the units it gained wait for a decision of their own, `claim:<id>+<units of the claim decided so far>` (listed, with a form, like the first); a repeated submit of it is still one decision, and it must cover exactly the units gained. A claim that shrinks asks for nothing (see the limits).
* **One parcel, however many forms.** A recorded return is keyed by the number of units already decided on the line when its form was opened, which is the same for every form opened at that moment: two people recording the one parcel that came back decide it once (the second finds its event decided), and a form opened before another decision was made is refused as stale if its position is no longer the line's. The key is derived on the server; no token is invented by the page.
* **Caps (enforced in the service, not only in the form).** A claim is decided for exactly the units it has not had decided (the two numbers must add up). A recorded return never exceeds what was sold less what was already decided, and is refused on a line that has claims (decide through the claim). Units put back never exceed what was taken from the cost layers for the line, over all of its returns. The location must be an active location of the order's seller. Nothing is put back when no cost of goods was recognised (the order has not shipped, or the cost module was off): writing off still works.
* **The cost that comes back is exact.** What a restock reverses is what the first N units cost, rounded once for N, so putting back every unit, in one decision or in several, reverses exactly the cost of goods of the sale and leaves nothing behind by rounding.
* **A sale cancelled after units were put back.** The cancellation gives all the units of the sale back to the layers they came from, so the layers the returns created are taken off the shelf (what is left of them) and the reversals of cost of goods they booked are cancelled with a settled zero: 10 bought, 3 sold, 1 put back, the order cancelled is 10 on the shelf and no cost of goods.
* **Audit.** Every decision writes an audit line (`operational_cost.return_decided`) with the line, the return event, the units, the location, who decided, and whether it was automatic. **The line is written by the platform's `AuditLogger`, which silently writes nothing when the platform's `audit_logs` table is absent, and no migration of this repository creates that table (it belongs to the platform's own database): on an installation without it the decisions are still recorded in `operational_cost_return_decisions` (who, when, how many, where), but there is no audit-log line.**
* **Permission.** The decision is an Operational Costs action wherever its form is shown. A role with only *Sales Channels* sees the returned units on the order page, without the form, and the route refuses it. The setting that makes the putting back automatic (below) is held to the same permission: the account pages need only *Sales Channels*, but a role without *Operational Costs* sees that switch as it is, not changeable, and its form cannot create, change or clear it.
* **Amazon.** The Amazon connector reads refunds but not returned units, so a return is recorded by a person on a shipped order (*Return received*, with the units to put back, the damaged units to write off and the location).
* **Automatic, the owner's decision.** The Trendyol account setting *Put units of accepted returns back on the shelf automatically* (`auto_restock_accepted_returns`) is **off by default**. When the owner switches it on, every unit of a claim the channel accepts (and every unit it accepts later) is put back at the location the line was sold from, at its original cost, damaged or not (only a person can tell a damaged unit), by the same service with the same caps and the same audit line, marked automatic. Only a role that holds *Operational Costs* can change the switch. It needs the master switch; a claim that cannot be decided yet (the cost of the goods was not recognised, no location on the line) stays on the list for a person and is tried again when the claim is read again.
* **Until it is decided** the unit counts in no stock and its cost of goods stays in the profit; the list of returns awaiting a decision is the worklist.
* **Store orders** are not part of this: the platform's own refund flow for store orders is left as it was (a store return reduces revenue; its units are not put back by this module), see the limits.

## Sources of truth: what the platform owns and what this module derives

The owner's instruction was to avoid keeping one fact twice and to unify the financial and inventory sources of truth without disturbing the running system. The rule that follows: **the platform's tables stay the truth and are only ever read; what the cost module keeps about them is derived from them, never the other way round, except where the platform has nothing.** A test (`PlatformTablesStayThePlatformsTest`) reads every file of both modules, wherever it lives (their directories, their models, commands, provider and middleware, their migrations, routes and views), and fails if any file writes a table that is not its own except the dispatch writes it lists, if a file names a commission, wallet or settlement table, if a migration of the modules names a table of the platform, or if a model of the platform is named anywhere but in the few files that read with it. It reads text, not behaviour (`tests/Support/PlatformWriteScanner.php`): a table named by a string, a constant, an alias, `from()` or a variable, a builder kept in a variable and written on a later statement, a join update, raw SQL (also with the table interpolated), the schema builder, and the platform's models made, bound, fetched and saved on another statement or written through a relation are all seen; what it cannot resolve it names "a table named in a variable", which the list has to carry. It cannot see a write made by code outside these files (the platform's own services the modules call) or built from strings at run time.

**What is inferred, and must be checked.** The platform's `suppliers`, `purchase_orders`, `purchase_order_items`, `warehouses`, `warehouse_stock`, `stock_movements` and `product_batches` have **no migration in this repository**; they come from the installation dump (the platform's own compatibility command says so). Everything this section says about their columns is inferred from the models (`$fillable`, `$casts`) and from the services that write them (`PurchaseOrderService`, `InventoryService`, `WarehouseService`), never compared with a real database. Every read is behind a check that the table and the columns are there; if one is not, the feature does nothing and names what is missing. The tests build the tables with `tests/Support/BuildsPlatformProcurementSchema.php` from the same inference and push receipts through the platform's own `PurchaseOrderService`; they prove the module's logic against a plausible shape, not against production. **Rehearse on a restored copy of the real database (see [rehearsal-on-a-copy.md](rehearsal-on-a-copy.md)) and run `php artisan costs:reconcile-platform` there before anything is switched on.**

### Reconciliation report: `php artisan costs:reconcile-platform`

Read-only (it runs nothing but SELECT statements, a test records them), needs the master switch, takes `--json`, `--limit=` and `--product=`. It shows, computed on demand and kept nowhere: per product, the units the open layers hold against the platform's stock plus the store-order units that already left that stock and are not delivered yet (the store takes units off at placement, the layers are taken from at delivery; the units channel orders took are shown beside the difference, because the platform's stock never saw them); per product, the units received on purchase orders against the units layered from them; the received lines no layer covers yet and the ones that never can be, each with its reason; lines whose received quantity fell below what was layered; the platform stock no layer covers; and negative platform stock. It changes nothing it finds. A column of the platform's store orders or of its product names that is not there is skipped and named, never an error (`skipped` in the JSON: the store orders are left out of the comparison). With `--json` both commands answer in JSON even when they refuse (`{"available": false, "message": "…"}`). Reasons a line is not layered: `no_product`, `vendor_order`, `vendor_product`, `not_physical`, `variant_product`, `m5azn_assigned`, `cost_missing`, `currency_unknown`, `no_exchange_rate`, `no_location`, `date_uncertain`, `covered_by_other_layers`.

### Layers from purchase orders: `php artisan costs:layers-from-purchase-orders`

* **Switch** `operational_costs.sources.purchase_orders` (`OPERATIONAL_COSTS_SOURCE_PURCHASE_ORDERS`), only the exact words count: `off` (default) reads and writes nothing; `shadow` only reports (`--dry-run`, which is also the default of the command) and writes no row anywhere, not even to the module's own tables (a test records every statement and the row count of every table; the one thing outside the database it can do is let the platform's own settings reader fill the platform's settings cache when that is cold, as any page of the store does, because the store's currency source asks it for the currency model, and that file belongs to the Torod Point / M5AZN workstream, which this work does not touch); `on` also lets `--apply` make layers, makes a linked invoice line record a variance instead of a second layer, and makes the latest order cost an estimate. `on` needs the master switch too. Neither command is on the schedule, whatever the switches say: the history the platform already holds needs a **cutover date** and an opening balance, and that is the owner's decision, not a timer's.
* **A receipt is a cumulative quantity.** The platform keeps no row per receipt, so a line is layered one *tranche* at a time: the units received since its last layer. Received 3 and later 7 is two layers; received 10 at once, one. The layer's identity is `po-item:{line}:{from}-{to}`, its `source_ref` the order's reference, and the layer table is **unique on `(source_type, source_id, source_from)`**, so two runs that overlap cannot both make the layer of the same units. A real multi-process test on MySQL 8, MariaDB 10.11 and MariaDB 11.4 shows it: two runs that saw the line at different moments (0-3 and 0-10) give one layer, and the same test with the key removed makes two. The units a line's layers account for are counted from the layers themselves, not from the two columns alone: the larger of where their tranches end and the units they hold, and for a layer that lost both columns (the migration was rolled back and applied again) the units found by trying the identities its name can have been made from, so a rollback does not bring a second set of layers (tests roll the migration back and run again, with a cutover inside a receipt too).
* **A dry run says what `--apply` will do**, also on a fresh install: the default stock locations that do not exist yet are counted as there (the real run makes them first, and only after it has checked that the platform's tables are all there: with one missing it makes nothing at all, not even a location). The listed layers show the place as `default` then.
* **Dated** from the platform's stock ledger when the receipts it recorded for the order and product add up to exactly what the line received (the date of the last one); otherwise the date is the line's last change and the layer is marked approximate (`received_at_basis`). **Cutover:** `--since=YYYY-MM-DD` leaves the units received before it to the opening balance; a line made on or after the date received nothing before it; a line that existed before, changed after, and whose receipts cannot be dated apart is refused (`date_uncertain`). `--apply` is refused without `--since`.
* **Only what may be layered is layered.** A catalogue product of the platform's own (a vendor's order or product is the vendor's), physical, with no variants (an order line names no variant, while a sale takes its units from the layers of its own variant), not assigned to M5AZN (its cost is the supplier's price per order), with a unit cost above zero (the order form writes 0 for a missing cost), a currency that is recognisable and has a rate to the reporting currency (a blank currency is refused, not assumed), and a place to put it. Anything else is left alone, listed with its reason, and **never made at zero**.
* **Where.** The pool a sale will take the units from: Torod Point's warehouse for a product assigned to it, the own warehouse for every other one; the same resolver (`StockLocationResolver`) the sale reader uses, pinned by tests that pass unchanged before and after it was introduced. Asking for the same units again at another location finds the layer that is there and makes no second one.
* **Cancelling** an order does not take received stock back in the platform, so the layers stay; nothing received means nothing made. A received quantity that falls **below** what was layered is reported (`received_below_layered`) and never reversed.
* **Purchase invoices.** A purchase line can name the purchase-order line it bills (`purchase_order_item_id`, a column on the invoice form): it makes **no second layer** (so the order line has to be able to make one: a link to a line the layers refuse, for a reason such as `cost_missing` or `variant_product`, is refused with that reason when the invoice is saved and again when it is approved, instead of leaving the goods with no layer at all; the way out is to correct the order line or to enter the line without the link and say it is a separate purchase) and records a cost variance against what the order says (a reconciliation row, basis `purchase_order`, accepted, disputed or written off by a person like any other). What the order says is its unit cost for the units of the line that no other **approved** invoice has billed, up to the units of this line (a line holds the units it received, or the units it ordered while the goods have not come): a second invoice for the same units, or one for more units than the line holds, is compared with what is left (nothing, or only the units that are there) and shows as a variance instead of a second "matched", and a supplier that bills in parts is matched part by part; the layer keeps the order's cost (changing a layer's cost after the fact is not built). A line that names none but is for a product and supplier of an order line that has **received more units than linked invoice lines have billed** (whatever its own quantity: a supplier bills in parts and in other quantities than it delivers) **holds the invoice** until a person links it or says it is a separate purchase (the existing *this is not a duplicate* button). The hold is decided when the invoice is saved **and again when it is approved**, so an invoice saved before the goods came is held if the order has received them by the time somebody approves it; a line a person has looked at is marked and is not held again. Links and holds exist only while the source is `on`; with it off or in shadow an invoice is costed exactly as it always was, and a linked invoice cannot be approved with the source off (the goods would have no layer at all).
* **The same goods are costed once** (audit fix). The invoice usually comes *before* the goods, and an invoice or a cost typed by hand can be approved before the order is received, when nothing holds it. So `--apply` does not make the **first** layer of a line while an invoice layer (`purchase_invoice`) or a typed layer (`manual`) of the same product, in the platform's own stock, dated on or after the cutover, from the same supplier (or from one nobody can tell apart: a name that is missing, or the same name spelled in another case) already exists: the line is refused with `covered_by_other_layers`, listed (`covered lines: …`, also `covered_lines` in `--json`) and counted, and nothing is made. A person who has looked and knows it is a separate purchase names the line, `--include=12,15` (a dry run takes it too, to show what it would do), and the ids are written to the audit line (`included_lines`). Opening balances, transfers and returned units never cover a line; a line the order already layers is not looked at again (a person decided when the typed cost was entered). On the product-cost screen a cost typed for goods an order received is refused the same way until the person ticks *This is a separate purchase*. The rule errs on the side of refusing, so a product that regularly has typed costs besides its orders will need the line named each time; a covered line whose invoice is for only part of the order's units is better **linked** (the invoice line names the order line when it is saved) than layered twice. Limits: only an invoice or a typed cost made **after** the order was received is caught when the invoice is saved or the cost is typed (the hold and the screen's refusal look at what the order has received); one made before is caught when the order is layered; whatever a person releases or includes is on their decision.
* **Freight on a purchase invoice** (`inbound_freight` lines; there is no separate duty component, so duty is entered the same way) is shared over **all** the goods lines of the invoice by value (equally when they are worth nothing), whether a line makes a layer of its own or is linked to an order line. The share of a line that makes a layer goes into that layer's landed cost, as before. The share of a **linked** line is recorded as an `inbound_freight` cost entry (actual, unallocated, `calc_inputs.outside_layers`) on the freight line, because the order makes that layer and changing a layer's cost after the fact is not built; so is the whole freight line of an invoice that has no goods lines (with the source off such an invoice is costed as it always was, and its freight line is recorded nowhere). Nothing of a freight line is dropped: the layers' shares and the entries add up to the line, and the freight line says (`entry_id`) where its amount went. Before this, the freight of a mixed invoice was put wholly on the unlinked goods, and the freight of a fully linked invoice, or of one with no goods lines, was in no layer and no entry.
* **Estimates.** With the source on, the cost of the latest received order line that may be layered (converted to the reporting currency) is an estimate (`last_purchase_order`) ahead of `products.purchase_price`, and after the average of the open layers.
* **Supplier.** `supplier_id` (no foreign key: the platform hard-deletes suppliers) and the name as it was are kept on the layer and on an invoice; a transferred layer keeps them; a cost entry made from layers carries them in `calc_inputs.pieces`. The pieces of a layer with no supplier are exactly what they were, because an entry's identity is a hash of them. A test deletes the supplier, its orders and their lines and shows the layer and the entry still say who it was.
* **Vendor goods on a channel order.** If a channel order was made into an internal order (dispatch), a line whose goods are a vendor's (the internal line says `seller_is` other than `admin`, or the product is a vendor's) is never costed from the platform's layers or estimated: its cost of goods is *missing* with the reason `vendor_owned_goods`.

**Decisions that remain the owner's:** the **cutover date and the opening balance** (the layers made for goods received before the cutover, and for stock on hand at it, are entered as opening stock; replaying all history would count units that were sold long ago); how **shrinkage** is treated (a negative adjustment, an expiry write-off or a return to the supplier lowers the platform's stock and leaves the layers too high; the report shows it, nothing consumes layers for it); whether the **order's cost may stand in for `products.purchase_price`** as the estimate; a purchase order with a **blank currency** (refused today; assuming the store's currency would be a decision); the treatment of **variant products**, which no order line can feed; and **the freight of a linked invoice line**: it is a cost entry of its own (an unallocated `inbound_freight` cost), not part of the cost of the goods sold, because the order's layer is not changed after the fact; whether to add it to that layer's landed cost, a change of a layer that is not built, is for the owner to decide.

### Limits and open questions of the purchase-order source

What a test proves is said in the sections above. These are the things it does not, or that are limits on purpose; the first two are known and not built, the rest are for the owner to decide or to check on the restored copy.

* **A layer that spans several receipts is dated by the last one.** Receipts the platform logged between two runs of `--apply` are made into ONE layer (the units received since the last layer), dated by the last of them, so the units that came in earlier are used, first in first out, as if they had come in later by the length of the gap. Run the command after every receipt, or often, to keep one layer for each. One layer for each receipt is not built.
* **A layer made late is converted at the rate of the day it is made.** The store keeps one current rate for a currency and no history, so a dollar order layered three weeks after the receipt is booked at today's rate. The layer keeps the original cost and the rate it used (`original_unit_cost`, `fx_rate`), so a person can restate it; a rate history is not built.
* **The vendor-goods guard does not depend on the source switch.** A channel-order line whose goods are a vendor's is never costed from the platform's layers or estimated (its cost of goods is *missing*, `vendor_owned_goods`) with the purchase-order source off, in shadow or on. That is intended: the platform's layers are not a vendor's cost, and costing the line from them was wrong before the source existed.
* **The order's unit cost is taken as it is: net or gross of VAT is not known.** If the platform's order form takes prices with VAT, the layers carry it. Check on the restored copy how the form is used, and decide.
* **A layer stays where it was made.** The place comes from the product's assignment when the layer is made (Torod Point's warehouse, or the own one). If the product is assigned elsewhere later, its layers are not moved (moving stock between places is a transfer, which nothing does by itself).
* **Layers made now reach sales already recognised only when `php artisan costs:refresh --days=N` is run.** The schedule looks at the last three days. Sales recognised earlier keep their estimates until it is run for their period, whether the layers came from orders, invoices or hand-typed costs.
* **`layerTranche` has not been run inside someone else's transaction.** The command and the service call it outside one. The layer table's unique key is what makes two runs safe; on MySQL and MariaDB a duplicate key undoes only its own statement, so the recovery in `InventoryCostLayerService::receive` would work inside a transaction too, but no test exercises it there, and a lock wait or a deadlock inside a caller's transaction is not covered.
* **A very large `--limit` makes a very large audit line.** A run writes one audit line carrying the first `--limit` layers (50 by default); the size of `audit_logs.context` has not been checked against a large figure. Keep the default.
* **The dry run's idea of the default stock locations.** A dry run counts the default places (the own warehouse, Torod Point's) as there when they do not exist yet, because `--apply` makes them first. The set-up that makes them runs at most once an hour (the cache says so): if they were deleted within the hour of the last check, `--apply` cannot make them and refuses the lines with `no_location`.

## Known limits of the cost module

* **Volume.** A rule priced *by volume*, and a per-day rule that multiplies by the volume in m³, can be entered in a contract, but nothing in the system knows the volume of a shipment or of the stock, so each always records a *missing* cost (`volume_unknown`).
* **Receiving.** A `receiving` fee is accepted as a contract line, but no service evaluates it; it is recorded only from an invoice line.
* **Stock transfers.** Moving stock between locations without a sale (`InventoryCostLayerService::transfer`) is built and tested as a service but is called from no screen, command or event.
* **Returns are decided by a person.** Nothing puts a returned unit back by itself except the owner's own setting (Trendyol accepted claims, off by default). Until a return is decided its cost of goods stays in the profit; the list *Returns awaiting a decision* and the tile on the overview are the worklist. Amazon has no returns reader (its refunds are read, its returned units are not), so a person records each Amazon return. The person names the location the units go back to; the one the line was sold from is offered.
* **A decided return is not undone when the channel takes units back.** If the channel changes a claim after it was decided so that it accepts FEWER units (a claim withdrawn, an item no longer accepted), the decision stands and the units stay on the shelf; reversing a put-back (taking the layer and the reversal entry back) is not built, and the revenue follows the claim as the channel now reports it. A claim that accepts MORE units is not a limit: the units it gained are decided as a follow-up (above).
* **A returned unit that was sold again before the sale was cancelled** is counted twice: cancelling takes off what is left of the layer the return created, not the units other sales already took from it.
* **Store orders are left as they were.** `SaleCostService::restock` works from the cost layers a recognised sale line consumed and would accept a store line, but nothing in the platform's store refund flow says when a unit physically came back and that flow belongs to the platform, so no restock decision exists for store orders: a store refund reduces the revenue (as before) and its units are not put back by this module. Owner decision.
* **Amazon refunds were built from the documentation and a fixture, not from a real refund.** The sign (money back to the buyer is negative), the identifiers a refund carries, the item's SKU, and which breakdown type is the goods, the shipping, a promotion or the tax (`amazon.refund_parts`) are unverified. The line a refunded item belongs to is found by its SKU (or the only line of the order); an item that names no SKU on an order with several lines is kept for a person, never guessed onto a line. Check the first real refund against its order page.
* **Overlaps are marked, not resolved.** A statement row that names no order and no settlement, and meets several API charges of the same amount on that day (or none yet), is imported and counted and marked `possible_overlap` for a person; the API reading the period later only marks it too (the row was posted as a fee of no order, and taking that back is a person's decision). A charge the API reported with a different amount (a rounded figure) is not recognised as the same one.
* **A posted invoice line a person contested before its statements arrived** is in no total, so setting the line against the statements cancels nothing; if the dispute is later resolved as "the charge stands" the line counts again next to the statements. Resolve such a dispute with the statements in view.
* **A person's reversal of a statement fee after its invoice line was compared** is picked up when the next fee of that kind is posted, or when *Reconcile* is pressed on the invoice; until then the comparison shows what it showed.
* **Invoice lines are compared with the statements** only for the fee types of the marketplace class, of a connected channel, with a period (on the line or the invoice); the total is that of the effective actual costs the API and imported statements booked for the fee, currency and period (and the invoice's account, when it names one). A line the statements booked nothing for is posted as an actual, as before.
* **Scope of profit.** Sales of the platform itself (`orders.seller_is = admin`) and channel orders; store orders of vendors are outside costs and profit.
* **First profits.** Sales that existed before the deployment have no cost rows and show provisional profit until purchase invoices or opening stock (FIFO layers) are entered and `php artisan costs:refresh --days=365` has been run once; the schedule covers only the last 3 days.
* **Platform tables.** Received purchase orders can make cost layers (see *Sources of truth*) only when the owner switches the purchase-order source on and runs the command, and only for the platform's own physical products without variants; until then the cost is entered on the product-cost screen or an invoice as before. `inventory_locations` still run parallel to `warehouses` and read neither `warehouse_stock` nor `stock_movements`; the default own-warehouse location has no writer, so it stays empty (another workstream); commission rules, seller wallets and vendor settlements are deliberately not connected (separate tables, direction and scope; a test keeps it so). The platform's procurement and stock tables are **inferred, not verified against a dump**.
* **Invoice allocation.** A shipping charge shared by several items is split on the basis the *estimate* used (the contract's `allocation_basis` setting, which has no field on the contract screen); an invoice is always split on the configured default (`operational_costs.allocation`).
* **Storage.** It is costed from the stock figures the location holds when the command runs (there is no stored figure per day: running `--date` for an earlier day prices it with today's stock), shared over at most `operational_costs.storage.max_allocation_rows` (5,000) products, beyond which the charge stays unallocated. A location that holds nothing and that no contract prices (a default location nobody uses) records nothing; one that holds stock but has no contract records a missing cost (`no_contract`); one with a contract but no trustworthy figure, a missing cost (`stock_unknown`). A day with trustworthy figures for only some products is costed on those products' units and recorded as an estimate without a missing flag for the others.
* **Reconciliation decisions** are *accept*, *dispute* and *write off*. For a line compared with an estimate, *dispute* and *write off* leave the billed charge as it is in the ledger, with the note in the audit trail; for a line compared with the statements, *accept* and *write off* book the difference as an adjustment and *dispute* books nothing. There is no "keep the estimate", because approving an invoice stops its estimate from counting.
* **Invoices are entered by hand** (a typed table or pasted spreadsheet rows); there is no file upload, so the file-hash duplicate check the service has is reached by no screen.
* **Floats.** `Money` never uses floats, but a few boundaries do before converting: price × quantity of a store order line and the sums of refunds (`SaleDocumentReader`), the VAT divisor of a Trendyol line, and the price sent to a channel (rounded to two decimals).
* **Reports** read at most 5,000 sales (1,000 per product) and say so when they stop there.
* `Money` uses `brick/math`, which comes with `laravel/framework` and is not declared in `composer.json`.

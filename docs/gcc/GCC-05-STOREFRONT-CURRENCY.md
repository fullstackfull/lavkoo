# GCC-05: the currency of a storefront

Status: slices 1 to 4 of 5, in draft pull requests, behind one switch that is off by default (`GCC_STOREFRONT_CURRENCY`). Nothing here has been run on the pilot server or on production.

## The rule

A storefront other than the default one trades in its own currency (`gcc.storefronts.countries.XX.currency`). The prices of its catalog are entered and shown in that currency, and nothing is converted from another storefront's amounts. This is the owner's "local pricing, no automatic conversion" decision (`DECISIONS.md`) applied to money display. Before this slice the platform had one currency model for everything: prices were stored in a base currency (USD) and converted for display with the visitor's currency, so a Kuwaiti product row showed the Saudi default currency's symbol and rate (`docs/gcc/audits/2026-11-16-price-stock-map/`, finding 1).

## What slice 1 does

While `GCC_STOREFRONT_CURRENCY` and `GCC_STOREFRONTS_ENABLED` are both on, and a request is served from a storefront other than the default one, `getWebConfig()` answers three business settings for that storefront alone (`App\Services\Storefront\StorefrontCurrency`):

| Setting | Platform value (unchanged for the default storefront) | Value for the other storefronts |
|---|---|---|
| `system_default_currency` | the platform's default currency | the id of the storefront's currency in the `currencies` table |
| `currency_model` | `single_currency` or `multi_currency` | `single_currency`: an amount is shown as it is stored, with no rate |
| `decimal_point_settings` | the platform's decimals | the decimals of the currency: three for KWD, BHD and OMR, two for the rest (`gcc.storefronts.currency.decimals`) |

Every converter, symbol and formatter that already asks `getWebConfig()` follows (`webCurrencyConverter`, `usdToDefaultCurrency`, `currencyConverter`, `setCurrencySymbol`, `loadCurrency`, the payment, order-edit and fulfillment readers), without a change of its own. The values are computed per call and never written to the settings table or to the settings cache, so the platform's own values stay what they were for every other request.

What does not change: the default storefront (Saudi Arabia unless the admin changes it), every request while either switch is off, and every process with no storefront bound (queue workers, console commands), which keep reading the platform settings.

### A storefront may sell only while its currency exists

A storefront whose currency is missing from the `currencies` table, or switched off there, keeps the platform settings (it never shows another storefront's rate) and **may not sell**: `StorefrontSellingGate` refuses a new order or payment with the same answer as for missing legal texts, and `GET /api/v1/storefronts` lists it with `sellable: false`. The default storefront needs no currency of its own.

### Readiness check

```bash
php artisan gcc:storefront:currency-readiness
```

Read-only. Lists every configured storefront, its currency, decimals, whether it is switched on, and whether its currency is ready, missing or inactive. Exits 1 when a storefront that is switched on lacks its currency.

## What slice 2 does: the order records what it was sold in

An order has no currency of its own: invoices, e-mails and exports recompute its totals with whatever the platform settings are when they are read, so a Kuwaiti order cannot be shown later in the currency it was sold in. Slice 2 adds the record (`App\Services\Storefront\OrderStorefrontSnapshot`, migration `2026_11_26_100000_add_storefront_snapshot_to_orders_table.php`).

| Column of `orders` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `storefront_country` | the one country the products of the order's lines belong to (null for an order whose lines belong to two) |
| `currency_code` | the currency the amounts of the order are in |
| `currency_rate` | the rate that turns a stored amount into that currency: 1 for a storefront with its own currency, because its amounts are stored as they are |

It is written right after the order's lines are saved, from those lines and not from the request, so the web and API checkout (`OrderManager::generateOrder`, which both POS screens use too) and the seller app's POS record the same thing. A record is written once and never rewritten. The default storefront on a multi-currency platform records its storefront only: the currency an order is shown in there is the visitor's choice, which an order does not know yet. An order saved before the migration, or while the switch is off, carries no record and is read as before (default storefront, platform settings). The migration is additive (three nullable columns appended without a position), and its rollback is refused while any order carries a record.

## What slice 3 does: a payment is refused before it starts when its gateway cannot take the storefront's currency

The amount of a storefront with its own currency is in that currency, so a gateway has to take that very currency: it is never converted to dollars, and never goes to a gateway whose list of currencies (`App\Traits\PaymentGatewayTrait`) does not have it. A currency with three decimals (KWD, BHD, OMR) goes only to a gateway somebody has verified for it, because most gateways multiply the amount by one hundred and would ask for a tenth of the price (`gcc.storefronts.currency.verified_three_decimal_gateways`, empty at first: a payment in such a currency is refused). `App\Services\Storefront\GatewayCurrencyPolicy` asks in `Payment::generate_link()`, which every payment starts in (the checkout, the wallet top-up and the payment of an edited order's due), so a refused payment leaves no `payment_requests` row. The answer is 422 with the code `storefront_payment_not_supported` for an app, and a message for a browser (a new text in `en` and `sa`, to be read by a native Arabic reader). Cash on delivery, the wallet, offline payments, callbacks, refunds and the payments that were already started are not affected, and nothing is refused while the switch is off, for the default storefront, or outside a storefront request.

## What slice 4 does: the admin product screens work in the money of the product's country

`StorefrontCurrency::within($country, $callback)` puts one market's money in force for the duration of a callback, whatever the request is narrowed to (the panels see every country, so nothing narrowed them before). The middleware `money.market` (`ScopeMoneyToMarket`) is on the admin product routes of the catalog group: the market is the country the form names (`storefront_country`, a new product) or else the stored country of the product the route names (`{id}`: view, edit, update, delete, barcode). Inside, the conversions of the product screens (`currencyConverter`, `usdToDefaultCurrency`, the symbol and the decimals) are those of that country's currency, so an amount typed for Kuwait is saved as typed (3.505 dinars, not 3.505 divided by the rate of the riyal) and read back as dinars with three decimals. The admin product list shows each row's price in its own country's currency, so a list that mixes countries never prints a dinar amount with the riyal's symbol. The five picker and search routes that span every country are outside the group and name no one market. Nothing changes for a product of the default storefront, for an unknown country, or while the switch is off; the market in force before is restored even when a save throws.

## What slice 5 does so far: the amounts a storefront compares with, and shipping

The amounts of money that the cart, the checkout and the wallet compare with a customer's amounts are rows of the platform's settings, typed in the platform's money. A storefront that trades in its own currency reads them through the same seam as the currency settings (the hook in `getWebConfig` already sent every name in `StorefrontCurrency::OVERRIDDEN_SETTINGS` to the service, and `settings.php` and every reader are unchanged):

- `minimum_order_amount` and `free_delivery_over_amount` answer what an operator gave that storefront, verbatim, in its own currency, or `0` (no minimum, no free delivery). The amounts live in one business setting per storefront, `storefront_money_<country>` (for example `storefront_money_kw`), a JSON object tagged with the currency: `{"currency":"KWD","minimum_order_amount":"5.000","free_delivery_over_amount":"50.000"}`. The tag has to equal the currency the storefront is configured to trade in and an amount has to be a plain number that is not negative; a row for another currency, a broken row and a bad amount read as nothing given and are never converted.
- `free_delivery_over_amount_seller`, `minimum_add_fund_amount` and `maximum_add_fund_amount` always answer `0` on such a storefront. A maximum top-up of zero refuses every top-up: the wallet is one balance for every storefront until the per-market wallets exist (GCC-15), and a balance in one currency may not be topped up in another.
- The one writer is `php artisan gcc:storefront:money {country} {setting} {amount?} {--unset}`: only the two amounts, only for a configured storefront that is not the default one, only a plain non-negative number with at most the decimals of the currency (three for KWD, BHD and OMR, two for AED and QAR), normalised and tagged, changed inside a transaction that locks the row, dropping the settings cache, never touching a platform row. It works while the switches are off and then says nothing reads the value yet. The amount screens of the admin panel are not the way: they type in the platform's money and would divide an amount by the exchange rate.
- `php artisan gcc:storefront:currency-readiness` lists, for each storefront that is switched on and is not the default one, what it was given and what it was not, and warns (it never fails) about a row tagged with another currency.
- **Shipping.** The platform's shipping tables are in the platform's money, so while a storefront's own currency is in force every read of `ShippingType::shipping_type` answers `product_wise`: shipping is the cost typed on each product, in that storefront's currency, and nothing from a method, category or zone table reaches a total. The stored value is untouched, and the in-house type is cached raw so that one Kuwaiti request never fills the Saudi entry.
- The flat referral discount (an amount stored with a dollar label) is `0` while a storefront's own currency is in force; the percentage discount is unchanged.

The default storefront, a process that serves no storefront (a panel, a queue, the console, the seller APIs) and every storefront while a switch is off read the platform rows exactly as before. Tests: `StorefrontAmountSettingsTest` (the platform amounts for the default storefront, no storefront and the switches off, pinned on the unchanged seam first; the five storefronts' own amounts and neutral values; the tag, the broken and the negative rows; the row name), `StorefrontMoneyCommandTest` (the writer, its refusals, the readiness lines) and `StorefrontShippingTypeTest`.

- **The panel edit of an order.** A panel sees every country, so an edit of a Kuwaiti order used to compare its dinars with the platform's minimum order, apply the Saudi free-delivery threshold, price shipping from the platform's tables and print the platform's symbol. The three reads of `OrderEditManager` (`checkEditOrderMinimumOrderAmount`, `checkFreeDeliveryOrderAmountArray`, `getEditOrderTotalShippingCost`) now run in the money of the order: `StorefrontCurrency::withinOrder` puts in force the storefront its record names, or the default storefront for an order that has none (a legacy order never takes the money of whichever storefront the editor's own request is served from). Their signatures and callers are unchanged.
- **A coupon belongs to one storefront.** A coupon is three numbers with no unit (the discount, the minimum purchase, the cap), typed in one currency, so only the storefront whose currency it was typed in may offer it. `coupons.storefront_country` (migration `2026_12_01_100000`, a nullable `char(2)` appended without a position, no index, `ascii_bin` on MySQL and MariaDB) is NULL for the default storefront's coupon, in the platform's money, which is every coupon that exists today (no row is touched), and a country code for a coupon typed in that storefront's currency, as typed, at rate one. Its rollback is refused while a coupon names a storefront. The column is fillable and hidden, so the apps and the seller API get a coupon byte for byte as before.
- **The read side.** `Coupon::forStorefront()` (`CouponCountry::narrow`) keeps the coupons the storefront may offer: its own, and for the default storefront also those with no country. While the currency switch is off nothing is narrowed and no schema is probed; a process that serves no storefront (a panel, a queue, the console) works in the default storefront's money; before the migration a storefront with its own currency meets no coupon at all and the default storefront meets all of them. It is asked by the two eligibility lookups of `OrderManager` (the web and API coupon apply, the settlement of a paid payment, the cart preview), the REST coupon list, applicable list and seller-wise list, the coupons of a seller on a shop page, the coupon strip of the home page, my coupons and the in-house coupons of the top vendors. Not scoped on purpose: the preview that reads a coupon's type, the reward snapshot read by code after the decision, and the coupon of an order edit, which finds the order's own coupon by its unique code. The condition `min_purchase > eligible total` and the admin form's rule `min_purchase > discount` are not touched.
- **The write side.** The admin routes of coupons run under `money.market:coupon`: a new coupon is typed in the money of the storefront the form names, an existing coupon in the money of the storefront it already has (whatever a form says, because a coupon's amounts are typed in one currency and its storefront never changes), so `currencyConverter` keeps an amount as typed and `usdToDefaultCurrency` shows it as typed. A new coupon is stamped by `CouponService::getAddData` through `CouponCountry::attributesOfNewCoupon` (nothing while the switch is off or the column is missing; NULL for the default storefront, for no choice and for a country that is not a storefront). The add request validates the field: a configured country, and for a storefront with its own currency one whose currency is active (a coupon typed for a storefront with no active currency would be stored as typed and read in the wrong money). The update never carries a storefront. Inside a storefront's money `usdToDefaultCurrency` removes the binary noise before it floors (1.005 is `1.00499999999999989…`, so a plain floor at three decimals showed 1.004 and the next save of the edit form kept it); outside a storefront the arithmetic is the platform's own, unchanged.

Tests of these: `StorefrontOrderEditMoneyTest` (13), `CouponStorefrontMigrationTest`, `StorefrontCouponEligibilityTest`, `CouponFindersAreScopedTest` and `StorefrontCouponAdminTest` (25: the market of a new and of an existing coupon, the form that names another storefront, the switch off, every admin coupon route carrying the middleware, what is saved for each storefront, the validation, the whole add and update routes, the edit-form rounding for amounts that lose a digit, the platform's arithmetic characterised outside a storefront).

Still to do in slice 5: the country select and currency labels on the admin coupon forms (without them the panel cannot yet type a Kuwaiti coupon), the amounts the admin coupon list, quick view and export show for a coupon of another storefront, the final lines of the readiness command, and the tax and refund snapshot (see the list below).

A finding for the owner, not changed here: on a single-currency platform the same floor loses a digit for 287 of the 5,000 two-decimal amounts from 0.01 to 50.00 on the platform's own screens (for example 0.29 shows as 0.28 and 1.15 as 1.14), so editing and saving such an amount in the Saudi panel changes it by one halala; on a multi-currency platform the stored base-currency amount makes the same loss likelier. Changing it alters the Saudi panel's arithmetic, which needs the owner's approval.

## What is not done yet (slice 5, and what slice 4 leaves)

Do not turn the switch on with a non-Saudi catalog before the bulk tools below know a market.

Left by slice 4: the bulk import (no country on the import form: it always lands in the default storefront), the bulk price operations and the other product lists (stock limit, restock requests, updated products, the gallery offcanvas), and every other screen that types or shows an amount of one country: coupons, the order edit of the panels, promotions (GCC-17); the shipping costs, the minimum order and the free-delivery thresholds are done above, and the vendor and seller tools (all Saudi until sellers have markets).

5. **Wallets, loyalty, fixed decimals, documents and the apps.** Wallet and loyalty balances have no currency (GCC-15) and two wallet paths feed a base-unit amount into a function that treats it as default-currency (an existing bug of the wallet stack, independent of the switch); a few formatters round to two decimals whatever the currency (`BackEndHelper::usd_to_currency`, `getFormatCurrency`); the MyFatoorah money class is two-decimal by definition; invoices, order e-mails, exports and reports read the order's own record; queue jobs carry the storefront they work for; `GET /api/v1/config` answers for the storefront an app asks for; the customer app applies the currency and decimals of the storefront.

## Rollback

Switch `GCC_STOREFRONT_CURRENCY` off (and clear the config cache). No data was written by slices 1 to 4, so nothing has to be repaired: every storefront reads the platform settings again at once. Slice 5 writes only what an operator chooses to give with `gcc:storefront:money` (`storefront_money_<country>` rows, ignored while the switch is off); removing them is `--unset`, or deleting the rows.

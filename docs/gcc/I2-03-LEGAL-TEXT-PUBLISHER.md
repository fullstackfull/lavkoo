# I2-01 and I2-03: how the legal text of a storefront is written

Status: coded and tested on SQLite and on MySQL 8.0.46 (branch `gcc/p1-legal-text-publisher`, stacked on the legal review of PR 105; no MariaDB run). It needs **no migration** (it writes the table `storefront_legal_documents`, whose migrations are already in the stack) and **no switch**: nothing reads a text of a storefront other than the Saudi one until storefronts are on, and the service is called by the command and by the admin screens (`docs/gcc/I2-STOREFRONT-ADMIN-SCREENS.md`). Nothing is MET until CI has run on a pushed head.

## What was missing

The readiness of a storefront (`StorefrontLegalReadiness`) reads the newest effective published row of each required document type and locale, and counts it only when someone other than its author approved it, it is not revoked, not blank and not an unedited template. **Nothing wrote those rows**: the table was filled by hand, so no new country could ever have the texts its readiness asks for through the platform, and an approval, a publication or a revocation left no trace.

## The publisher

`App\Services\Storefront\StorefrontLegalPublisher` is the only writer of the table. Every step is written to the audit log in the same transaction (a step whose trace cannot be written is not taken), with who took it (the id of the admin), the storefront, the type, the locale and the version, the state before and after, and the **hash** of the words (never the words).

| Step | What it does | What it refuses |
|---|---|---|
| `draftFromTemplate` | a draft that is a copy of a template; its `template_hash` is the hash of the template, so that the template unedited can be told from a text | a blank template, a country that is not a storefront, the default storefront, a type or locale that is not shaped like one, a second unpublished text of the same type and locale |
| `draftNewVersionOf` | the next version of the **newest** published text of its storefront, type and locale, as a draft that starts as a copy of it; the published text stays in force. The copy carries the hash of the words it was copied from as its `template_hash`, so a copy nobody edited is refused at approval exactly like an unedited template (readiness already counts neither) | a text that is not published; a **revoked** text (its replacement is written from a template, because its words were revoked as wrong); a text that is **not the newest published** one (a newer published text, scheduled or in force, supersedes it; drafts, discarded texts and texts revoked before they took effect do not count); a second unpublished text |
| `edit` | changes a draft; an **approved** text goes back to draft and its approval is void; the editor is its author now | a published, revoked or discarded text (a published text is never edited in place); a blank text |
| `approve` | draft to approved; with `expectedBodyHash` (see below) only if the words are the ones the approver read | the **author** (the last person who changed it), a text that is still its template (or the text it was copied from), a blank text, a text that is not a draft, a text whose words changed after they were read |
| `publish` | approved to published, from now or a later day; with `expectedBodyHash` only if the words are the ones the publisher read | a day in the past (five minutes of tolerance), a day **after 2037-12-31 23:59:59 UTC** (`effective_from` is a timestamp column, which MySQL keeps until 2038-01-19 03:14:07 UTC: the step is refused in words, not by the database), a day before another text of the same type and locale that is already scheduled (the newest by effective day wins, so a newer version must not lose to an older one scheduled for later), a text that is not approved, a text whose words changed after they were read |
| `revoke` | marks a published text revoked; **the row stays**, and while it is the text in force the storefront does not sell; a text that has **not taken effect yet** is archived instead (revoked before its day), because a revoked text that stayed published would be the newest text of its type and locale on its day and would close the storefront in the text it was meant to replace | a blank reason; a text that is not published or is already revoked |
| `discard` | puts a draft or approved text away (archived), kept as history | a published text (it is revoked) |

Nothing is deleted. The default storefront keeps the documents it has always had (GCC-19) and has no text here.

### An approval is bound to the words that were read

`approve()` and `publish()` take an optional `expectedBodyHash` (the sha256 of the words the person read, hex in either case). When it is given and is not the `body_hash` of the row **locked inside the transaction** (`hash_equals`), the step is refused with "changed after it was read ... read it again"; nothing is changed and nothing is audited. Without it the step behaves as before, so a caller that does not name a hash (the console command without `--hash`) is unchanged; the admin screen always names it, from the whole `body_hash` its approve and publish forms carry (`docs/gcc/I2-STOREFRONT-ADMIN-SCREENS.md`). A text edited between the reading and the approval, or between the approval and the publication (an edit sends an approved text back to draft), is therefore never approved or published on words nobody read.

## The command

`php artisan gcc:storefront:legal-text {list|draft|edit|approve|publish|revoke|discard} --admin=ID ...` is the entry point of the operator; the admin screen with its permissions is `docs/gcc/I2-STOREFRONT-ADMIN-SCREENS.md`. It reads a template or a text from a file (at most 1 MB, `StorefrontLegalTextCommand::MAXIMUM_FILE_BYTES`), reads the day as `YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM` in UTC whatever the time zone of the application is, says after each step what it did and whether the storefront's texts are complete now, and exits 1 with the reason in words when the service refuses. `list` shows the texts without their words. It works while the switches are off.

- **Files.** A UTF-8 byte order mark is dropped; content that is not valid UTF-8 (a Windows-1256 file, a UTF-16 file with or without a mark; a NUL byte is refused too) is refused with a message that says the file must be UTF-8, a directory, an unreadable file, a file over the limit and an empty file are refused, and nothing is sanitised: the text is stored as it comes.
- **The admin.** The id must be in the table of admins **and the admin must not be switched off** (`status`, the flag the admin login checks; admin 1 is not exempt, because the login requires the status for every admin). A step by an admin who is off is refused before anything is taken.
- **The hash.** `list` shows the first 12 characters of each text's `body_hash`; `draft`, `edit` and `approve` print the first 12 characters of the hash of the text they leave. `approve` and `publish` accept `--hash` (a hex prefix of at least 12 characters, or the whole 64, in either case): it is compared with the start of the text's hash and the step is refused when it does not match or when it is shorter than 12 or not hex; the whole hash is then handed to the service as `expectedBodyHash`, so the comparison is repeated under the lock. **Only leaving `--hash` out means "no hash"**: an empty `--hash=` or `--hash ""`, one of spaces, and a `--hash` with no value at all (the three things a script's command line gives when its variable is empty, quoted or not; the parser reads the third as null, exactly like an option that is not there, so the command asks the input whether the option was given) are refused like any other value that is not a hash (exit 1, the text left as it was, nothing audited), never read as no hash.
- **Draft.** An empty or missing `--country`, `--type` or `--locale` is refused with a message that starts with the option. `--new-version-of` follows the rule of `draftNewVersionOf` above.
- **Revoke.** A text revoked before it took effect is archived: the command says so, and that the text in force stays in force. Otherwise it says that the storefront does not sell while the text is in force and that the replacement is written from a template (`draft --template-file`, then edit, approve and publish), because a new version of a revoked text is refused.

## What it does not do

- **The command has no permission gate.** The person is the admin id typed by whoever runs the command (an id that exists, not a name). The screen has its permissions and takes the person from the session (`docs/gcc/I2-STOREFRONT-ADMIN-SCREENS.md`); a step-up or a dual approval for publishing and revoking (I2-03 criterion 5, GCC-20 level 3) is still not built.
- **No template source.** A template is a file the operator names. The Saudi business pages are not read as templates yet.
- **No retention rule** for texts and acceptance rows (I2-01 criterion 2): nothing here deletes a row, and no rule says how long they are kept.
- **No Saudi baseline** (I2-01 criterion 1): the default storefront keeps its pages as they are.
- A new version does not withdraw the independent legal review of PR 105; whether it needs another one is the owner's decision.

What the two tests of the command with a second admin prove and what they do not: the second admin's step is simulated by a one-shot listener on the text model that runs the step through the service at the command's own read of the text, so they prove that the service repeats the comparison on the row it reads itself. None of them opens two database connections: the row lock of the engine (which SQLite ignores) is not exercised by any test here. The test that feeds the command the words of a command line (an array of tokens that Symfony's ArgvInput parses through the console kernel, as `php artisan` does) is the only one that does not go through the array of options of `artisan()`: it proves how the four spellings of an empty `--hash` are read and refused, and it stops at the parser, with no shell and no separate process.

Eleven mutations of the service (the author may approve, a template approved as it is, a published text edited in place, a text in the past, a text before a scheduled one, a second open text, the editor not the author, the approval surviving an edit, the copy forgetting its template, the words in the audit line, revoke archiving the row) each fail the class, and so do eighteen more for this review (approve or publish ignoring the hash, the hash compared in one letter case, a revoked or a superseded source copied, a new version without its template hash, no upper bound on the day, the file encoding unchecked, NUL bytes unchecked, the byte order mark kept, an admin who is off acting, the day read in the time zone of the application, one revoke sentence for both cases, `--hash` ignored, a missing option not named, the list without its hash, and the file limit moved one byte either way), and so do seven more for the second review (an empty or blank `--hash` read as no hash again, the newest-version query of a new version ignoring a revoked newer text, the command handing the service nothing instead of the whole hash, which also fails when only the approval or only the publication does it, approve checking the hash after the status, admin id 1 exempt from the status check), and so do three more for the third review (a `--hash` with no value read as no hash again, because the option is tested for null instead of being asked of the input, and the same for the approval only and for the publication only; each fails its own tests).

## Decisions that remain with the owner

1. Whether publishing needs a second person or a step-up (I2-03 criterion 5); who holds the three permissions is decision 1 of `docs/gcc/I2-STOREFRONT-ADMIN-SCREENS.md`.
2. Where templates come from (the Saudi page of the same type, a file kept by legal counsel, or a library).
3. Whether a new version of a text needs a new independent review.
4. How long texts and acceptance rows are kept (I2-01 criterion 2).
5. Whether the rule of `--hash` (an option that is given and is empty, blank or has no value is refused, never read as absent) extends to the other options of the command. It is only on `--hash` now, because only `--hash` binds a step to the words that were read. Still read as absent, as the real parser delivers them: `publish --effective-from=` (also `--effective-from ""` and a bare `--effective-from`) publishes at once instead of from a day, and `draft --new-version-of=` (or a bare one) with `--country`, `--type`, `--locale` and `--template-file` drafts from the template instead of refusing. An empty filter of `list` is read as absent too, which only widens a read-only list.

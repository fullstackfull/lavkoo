# I2-03 and I2-11: the admin screens of a storefront

Status: coded and tested on SQLite and MySQL 8, strict included (branch `gcc/p1-storefront-admin-screens`, which contains the lifecycle of PR 111 and the legal text publisher of PR 114, with its review fixes, by merge; draft, nothing merged). It needs **no migration** and **no switch**: it writes through the two services that already exist, and it is reachable only by an admin whose role holds one of three new permissions, which no role holds until someone grants it. Nothing is MET until CI has run on a pushed head.

## What was missing

Both decisions that open a country were console commands: `gcc:storefront:state` (a storefront is marked ready, activated, paused, resumed) and `gcc:storefront:legal-text` (a text is drafted, edited, approved, published, revoked). The person was a name or an admin number typed by whoever ran the command, no role could be refused, and a country could not be run from the panel the business uses. The tracker therefore kept I2-11 criterion "every change is permission-gated" and I2-03 criterion "each step is permission-gated" missing.

## The screens

`/admin/storefronts` lists every configured storefront with its state (draft, ready, active, paused and the stop mode), the four things it needs before it can sell (legal texts, their independent review, the currency, the tax decision) as met or unmet, who last changed it and why, what its customers are told and when the stop should end. A draft with something unmet is offered no step; a draft with everything met is offered *mark ready*; a ready storefront *activate* (with the place where the approval is written down); an active one *pause* in one of three modes, with a message for the customers and an end in UTC; a paused one *resume* or *change how it is stopped*. The default storefront is shown as active without a record and can only be paused browse-only.

`/admin/storefronts/{country}/legal-texts` lists the text each required document type and language needs and the one in force today, then every version that was ever written with who wrote and approved it and the steps it can still take: a **draft from a template**, an **edit** (an approved text goes back to a draft and its editor becomes its author), **approve**, **publish** from a day (UTC) or now, **revoke** with a reason, a **new version** of a published text, and **put a draft away**. The default storefront keeps its documents and has no form.

- **A step is offered only where the publisher accepts it.** *Revoke* is offered on a published text that is not revoked; *new version* only on the newest published text of its type and language when that text is not revoked and no draft or approved text of that type and language is open (the publisher copies the newest published text, a revoked one included in "newest", and refuses a revoked text, a text that a newer one has superseded, and a second text while one is not published). The newest is read for each language on its own. A revoked text is offered nothing; an older published text that is still standing can only be revoked, and so can the newest one while a text of its type and language is being written; a text archived before it took effect does not count as the newest, and a draft that was put away is no longer open. The board is a convenience of the page and not a guard: a page that was read before another admin started a text still shows the button, and the publisher refuses the step in its own words.
- **Every day is printed in UTC**, with the suffix, in the table of what is in force and in the list of versions, so that it reads the same as the field it was typed in, whatever the time zone of the application is.
- **The day field has an upper bound**: a day after 2037-12-31 23:59 UTC is a validation message on the field (the bound is the publisher's own constant), not a failure of the database.

Every step is the step the console command takes: the same service, the same refusals in the service's own words (shown to the person, escaped), the same audit line in the same transaction.

## Who may do what

Three permissions in the role form, in a section of their own like Monitoring (`StorefrontPermissionService`):

| Permission | Steps |
|---|---|
| `storefront_lifecycle` | mark ready, activate, pause, resume |
| `storefront_legal_write` | draft from a template, edit, new version, put a draft away |
| `storefront_legal_approve` | approve, publish, revoke |

The master admin holds all three; no other role holds any until it is granted; a switched-off role holds none. The permission is read from the role on every request, so granting and withdrawing take effect at once (tested through the real role form). The list of storefronts is open to a role that holds any one of them, the legal texts to a writer or an approver, and every POST needs its own. Writing and approving are two permissions so that the person who writes a text is not the person who puts it in force; the publisher refuses the author as the approver in any case, so a master admin who wrote a text still cannot approve it.

The routes use `EnsureStorefrontPermission`, not `module:a,b`: the module middleware of a tree without the security set reads its first permission only, and a route open to a writer or an approver must not depend on which tree it runs in.

## What keeps it safe

- **The person is the signed-in admin.** The lifecycle record carries "name (admin number)" built from the session, a typed `by` is ignored (tested), and the audit line's actor is the admin guard (`actor_id`, `actor_name`).
- **An approval and a publication are bound to the words that were read.** The page shows the first 12 characters of a text's `body_hash`; its approve form and its publish form post the whole hash of the text as it was rendered (a hidden `body_hash`), the request requires it for those two steps as exactly 64 hex characters (either letter case), and the controller hands it to the publisher as `expectedBodyHash`. The publisher compares it with the row it **locks**: a text that was edited after the page was read (between the reading and the approval, or after the approval and before the publication, even when someone else has approved the new words in the meantime) is refused in the publisher's words ("changed after it was read ... read it again"), and nothing is changed and nothing is audited. A post without the hash, or with a prefix or a value that is not a hash, is a validation error.
- **A text is cleaned before it is kept.** The website shows a legal text without escaping, so what is typed into the screen goes through the platform's `HtmlSanitizer` (scripts, styles, forms, frames, event handlers and `javascript:` links are removed). The console command still stores a file as it is.
- **Everything typed is shown escaped**, and a text is shown in a `<pre>` as its source, never rendered.
- A database failure is not shown as a refusal (its message holds the statement and the values bound to it); only the services' own refusals are. A test fails the first write of each of the eleven steps with a `QueryException` shaped like a deadlock and asserts a 500 whose page and toasts hold neither the message, the statement, the driver's words nor a bound value, with no toast at all and no row or audit line changed.
- Every POST has a throttle bucket of its own family: `storefront-lifecycle` (ready, activate, pause, resume; 30 a minute), `storefront-legal-write` (draft, new version, edit, discard; 60) and `storefront-legal-approve` (approve, publish, revoke; 30). A test reads the route collection, requires exactly those eleven routes (a new one that changes something fails it until its bucket is named) and checks each bucket; another exceeds the lifecycle bucket and gets 429 while the write bucket still answers.
- The screens work while `GCC_STOREFRONTS_ENABLED` is off, as the commands do, because a storefront is prepared before it is switched on, and the page says so.

## Found on the way

A moment typed in UTC (the screen and the command read theirs as UTC) was stored by the publisher as it came and read back in the application's time zone (`Asia/Dhaka` by default), so a text scheduled a few hours ahead took effect hours early. Fixed on PR 114 (`9f099f97`) with a test that fails without it; this branch contains the fix.

## What it does not do

- **No step-up and no dual approval for publishing, revoking and activating** (GCC-20 level 3, I2-03 criterion 5): the screen refuses the author as the approver, which is the dual control of the text, and nothing more.
- **No scope by country.** An admin who holds a permission holds it for every storefront; the staff scope of axis G (a role limited to some countries) is not applied to these screens.
- **The page does not say why** a standing text has no *new version* button while a draft or an approved text of its type and language is open; that text is in the list of versions.
- No source of templates (the person pastes the template) and no Saudi pages as templates; no retention rule; no acceptance receipt.
- The side bar and rail entry exists in the v2 admin layout only (the classic layout is not rendered by the panel any more).
- A native reader has not read the 80 new Arabic strings (`resources/lang/sa` and `ae`); see `reviews/R5-native-arabic-reader.md`.

## Tests

`AdminStorefrontLifecycleScreenTest` (18), `AdminStorefrontLegalTextScreenTest` (42), `AdminStorefrontRoleFormTest` (3), `AdminStorefrontSafetyTest` (13) and `AdminStorefrontMenuTest` (6, SQLite only because the side bar reads tables of the whole panel), 82 in all: who may open each page and take each step (master admin, each of the three roles, a role with another module, a switched-off role), a refusal leaving nothing changed and nothing audited, the person being the signed-in admin and not a typed name, every step through the real routes with its audit line and actor, the author refused as the approver in the page and in the service, a second writer becoming the author of what they edit, the day read as UTC and never from the past, a revoked text closing a storefront that sold, scripts and event handlers removed, the default storefront and an unknown country, and both screens in every state in English and in both Arabic files with no key written to a language file.

The review fixes are pinned by 29 of them: **the hash** (the forms carry the whole hash and the post of the page as rendered takes the step, in either letter case; a stale approval and a stale publication, the latter both while the edited text is a draft and after someone else approved the new words, are refused with nothing approved, published or audited and work once the page is read again; seven kinds of missing or malformed hash are validation errors that change nothing), **the day in UTC** (with the application set to `Asia/Dhaka`, the table of what is in force and the list of versions both print the UTC minute that was typed with the suffix, and not the Dhaka reading), **the upper bound** (a day after 2037-12-31 23:59 UTC, three of them, is a validation error that changes nothing, the last minute of 2037 is accepted and stored as the instant typed), **the board** (two tests. One reads a world of thirteen texts in both languages step by step from the board and from the buttons of the page: only the newest standing published text of a type and language is offered a new version, the newest is read for each language on its own (refund is ahead in Arabic and shipping in English, and neither takes the new version of the other language away), a revoked text is offered nothing, an older text only a revocation, and a text archived before it took effect counts for nothing. The other walks through the real screen: a draft of another language and a draft of another type take nothing away; once a new version is started, and again once its draft is approved, the standing text is offered a revocation only, on the board and on the page, and a second new version is refused by the publisher with nothing written and nothing audited; when the open text is discarded the standing text is offered a new version again, and when a new version is published the older text can only be revoked and the new one is offered a new version), and **the safety behaviours** (`AdminStorefrontSafetyTest`: a failure of the database in each of the eleven steps, the eleven routes and their buckets and attempts a minute read from the route collection, and a 429 on exceeding one bucket with another still answering).

The first round recorded twenty-six mutations of the new behaviours, each failing these classes (an approval or a publication ignoring the hash, the hash optional for either step, a prefix or a non-hex value accepted, the form carrying the twelve-character fingerprint, the day not converted to UTC, the suffix missing in either place, no upper bound or one that is a year or two minutes off, a new version or a revocation offered where the publisher refuses it, "newest" ignoring revoked rows, crossing types and languages or counting archived rows, a database failure shown as a refusal or swallowed as a success, a bucket renamed or missing, and the lifecycle limit raised to 1000). An independent review of that round found that one of them did not hold: removing the language condition alone from the board's reading of the newest published text left every test green, because no world held published texts in two languages of one type (removing the type condition alone did fail). The second round pinned it and ran eleven mutations of the board, each restored and each failing `AdminStorefrontLegalTextScreenTest`: the language condition removed alone and the type condition removed alone; a new version offered while a draft or an approved text of its type and language is open (the open text ignored, only a draft counted as open, only an approved text counted as open, a text that was put away counted as open); and, on the board as it is now, five that change what the older, the archived and the revoked texts are offered (every standing published text taken for the newest, the newest ignoring revoked texts, the newest counting archived texts, a revoked text offered a revocation and a new version, an older standing text offered nothing).

More than eighty draft PRs are open against `fullstackfull/lavkoo` (the newest are #75 to #86; #71 to #74 are in sections 4 and 7) and nothing is merged or deployed. This page says which to read first, which go together, and what to do at deploy. Each PR body repeats its own rule at the top; if this page and a PR body disagree, the PR body is newer.

Deploy checklist for this set:

1. Production must run with `APP_MODE=live`. Outside it several codes are fixed. On the server's code (`698aa1e`) the `.env` value alone is not enough, because `config('app.mode')` does not exist there: it also needs the one-line config change of the security plan (decision 1).
2. The delivery-man app is not in these repositories. #62's third commit (`e9352502`) needs it to call `verify-otp` before `reset-password`; confirm that, or leave that commit out.
3. `php artisan migrate` runs two data-only migrations (`2026_11_26_100000` in #62, `2026_11_26_100100` in #58) that empty the codes in flight and the reset proofs of customers, delivery men and sellers, because rows planted by the old code would still work. 4. `php artisan optimize:clear`.

What people will notice: a reset signs the customer out of the app; two accounts holding one phone or e-mail cannot reset by it; a seller whose status changes signs in again.

## 7. Decisions that are the owner's

- **The three repositories are public** (checked 2026-11-18: not private, and the plan opens without signing in). The plan, this guide, the audits and the pull request bodies describe flaws that are not fixed on the server yet. Decide whether to make them private (decision 15 of the plan). Deploying stage 1 is the remedy; visibility only limits who reads about it, and it does not recall copies already made. Documents added from now on stay generic about how a flaw is used. The risks the owner has decided not to act on (no key rotation now) are in `docs/gcc/DEFERRED_RISKS.md`: open, not remediated, and not raised again at every batch.
- **Stage 0 of the hotfix** (PR 85, draft, cut from the baseline; three files): removes the maintenance routes a web request could trigger and the log calls that wrote one-time codes. It goes first. Its CI is green (SQLite on PHP 8.2 and 8.3, MySQL 8, and its overlay job builds). It is also the 26th commit of the hotfix branch (tip `fafa1a2e`, PR 78), so the all-at-once package is now 211 files; staged, stage 0 is applied first and the package of stage 4 is built from the tip of stage 3 plus stage 0 (plan, section 4, *By stage*). Applied in order on scratch copies of the baseline, no file drifts and the result equals the all-at-once one. PR 86 (CI evidence only) runs the hotfix tree on MariaDB 11.4.2 and on MySQL 8 (5,121 tests on each, green, on the tip before stage 0; the new head is running). Credentials that are tracked in the repository are an open risk that you chose to defer on 2026-11-18 (`DEFERRED_RISKS.md`, DR-1; `docs/integrations/security-findings.md`, SEC-15): this stage rotates nothing and does not make them safe, and making a repository private recalls nothing.
- `APP_MODE=live` (above) and when to deploy the security set: until then `main` is exposed to everything in section 1.
- Customer reset codes have no lifetime, on purpose: the same table holds the "choose your password" links panels and the point of sale send, which are used days later.
- Two sellers holding one phone cannot recover a password by SMS or Firebase; a unique phone rule on seller registration and profile updates, and a report of the existing duplicates, would remove it.
- `registration-with-otp` and `registration-with-social-media` create accounts with any phone or e-mail marked verified; what proves them at registration is a product decision.
- The staff of a vendor panel with `payouts.request` can still change the owner's bank information; a phone-only social provider cannot sign in by social login; `SellerStaff` tokens are not rotated when a shop is re-approved.
- `GET /api/v1/products/details/{slug}` still carries every reviewer's e-mail and phone for a visible product.
- For GCC: a phone stored without a country code is read as Saudi; admin and seller tools do not yet set or filter a product's country.
- **#52, round 4 (your comment on the PR, implemented): finance review before merge.** An order edit is now one transaction (a failed save leaves the order, its lines, stock, rewards and history as they were), and an order line whose product is gone is kept exactly as stored and counted in the total, or the whole edit is refused with a clear message (when the line carries tax, when the edit would change or remove it, or when the order holds it twice). For finance to confirm: the reward shares of the other lines in such an order are now worked out over the whole order (they differ from the old code), and an order whose such line carries tax can no longer be edited at all. Orders already saved with a dropped line are not touched and no repair script exists; two simultaneous saves of one order can still both succeed in turn (stock given back twice): closing that needs a row lock and a re-read inside the transaction.
- **#71 (GCC-20, platform admin API, level 1 read-only): security review before merge, as the charter requires (section 5).** What to read first: `AuthenticatePlatformAdmin`, `PlatformAdminTokenService::principalFor`, `PlatformAdminAuthenticator`, `PlatformAdminGate` and the three migrations. Yours to decide (doc section 10): MFA before release (TOTP at sign-in, or a step-up before any level 2 or 3 action); the token lifetime (480 minutes, no refresh); who holds a grant (the platform owner for every market, country managers as restricted viewers, and whether a restricted viewer sees the unattributed bucket); GCC-05 (an order records neither country nor currency, so no sales figure is reported until it does); alert thresholds (placeholders); an address allow-list or a separate host; the retention of the audit table. The grants migration cannot fail a deploy (it adds its foreign key to `admins` only when `admins.id` is `BIGINT UNSIGNED` on InnoDB, otherwise one log warning); revoke a grant before deleting its admin.
- **#79 and #80 (GCC-20 phase 2, stacked on #71 in that order, off by default): the second factor, sessions, grants that end, a tamper-evident audit trail.** #79: sign-in in two steps (a challenge, then a TOTP code or a recovery code; the codes and the secret are shown once on the operator's terminal, console only), idle timeout, at most five open sessions, a lockout and a per-address limit on the second step, every refusal the same 401. #80: capabilities and an end date on a grant, every audit line chained to the one before it with `audit:verify` and a confirmed `audit:prune`, and isolation tests that walk the route table (a platform token changes no other route; no other identity's credential opens a platform route). Both are dormant with the API; the contract is `docs/gcc/GCC-20-PLATFORM-ADMIN-API.md` (first commit of #79). Yours to decide (contract section 10): the MFA policy for restricted viewers and for levels 2 and 3, who keeps the recovery codes, the lockout (ten wrong codes lock the second step for 15 minutes, so a person who knows the password can keep a real admin out; there is no unlock command), audit retention and who records the head hash `audit:verify` prints, capability sets and end dates as policy. Before the switch: all nine migrations, `TRUSTED_PROXIES`, a shared cache store, a stable `APP_KEY` (rotating it makes factors unreadable), a fixed server and database time zone, the security review, and the seller app built against the contract (the app's step 2 is a separate draft).
